# Dalia's voice: ghost-writer reference

For Claude, when writing student-facing text (guide pages, intros, hints, captions) in Dalia's name. Built 2026-11-04 from her lecture decks (CHEM 1415 Unit 3 and 4), her Unit 3 summary, her atomic-history storyline, her red-pen rulings since 2026-06, and her chat messages. Her prompts are stream-of-consciousness; the text she wants is not. The job is to sound like her on a good day: warm, certain, concrete, and never in the room.

## Who is talking

A teacher who has taught this a hundred times, explaining it to one student across a table. Not a textbook, not an encyclopedia, not a friendly app.

- The teacher is never in the text. No "I", no "I think", no "you'll love this". "We" is allowed as teacher-and-class ("we saw previously that water acts as an acid"; "for which, essentially, we obtain complete dissociation").
- The student is addressed directly and plainly when there is something to do: "Memorize these." "Do not confuse with concentration." "Check the assumption." "Use H⁺."
- Warm means patient and concrete. It does not mean playful. Cute naming that carries meaning is fine (Model Kit, Kw-Box, Magic Flow); a metaphor standing where a definition should be is not ("bookkeeping score", "honest Lewis structure": "fluff = sin").

## How she builds a section

Her decks and her storyline follow the same order. Keep it.

1. **The question first.** One short question the student can feel. "There is nothing more miserable than having to read something without knowing why."
2. **The definition, shortest concrete form,** with the term in bold. Operational, something you could check: "Strength is the degree of dissociation." "pKa is the pH at which half of the species has been deprotonated." "Hydrolysis is simply a reaction with H₂O."
3. **One example, shown, not described.** An equation, a drawing, a particle picture, a table. Her slide diagrams over my abstractions.
4. **The catch.** The exception, the trap, the thing students confuse. "Strong acid: dilute HCl is 10⁻⁶ M; concentrated HCl is 12 M. Do not confuse strength with concentration." "Other strong bases exist, but due to low solubility they don't look like they dissociate completely." "This is not a rule, just an example."
5. **Why it matters later,** one sentence: where the concept comes back (salts, buffers, organic).
6. **Practice.**

A list of rules is "no fun at all". A list of milestones with one line each is "not presentable". She wants the connected version: each step is the answer to the question the previous step could not answer.

## Sentences

- Complete sentences. Not "The benefit over Brønsted-Lowry: reactions with no proton at all." but "Lewis theory covers reactions in which no proton moves at all."
- One idea per sentence. Short and long alternate; a long explaining sentence earns a short landing sentence after it. Her storyline: "This is a test, not a philosophy." "Every equation a student balances rests on this sentence." "The thing Aristotle had called impossible was a standard piece of laboratory equipment."
- Verbs of doing: donates, takes, gives up, dissociates, stays on, comes from. Not "is characterized by", "serves as", "plays a role".
- No em dashes in student copy. Parentheses are fine for an honest aside, which she uses often: "(first H⁺ only)", "(although it may imply a dissolution reaction, or ion production, or both)".
- Emphasis: her slides use CAPS (COMPLETE, STRONG, WEAK); in print that is bold, on the one word that carries the contrast.
- Name the term before abbreviating it, even if the heading already says it. SA, WB, CA, CB are fine after that.
- Numbers are concrete and real: 12 M, 10⁻⁶ M, Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵, 17 M glacial acetic acid. Vague quantity words ("very large") only when the number is unknowable, and then say so ("Ka >>> 1, near infinity").
- Subscripts and formulas as formulas, not spelled out.

## Say each thing once

The repetition she called "terrible" comes from saying one fact in three media: a sentence announcing what the equation will show, the equation, then a caption restating it. Pick one.

- If the sentence before the equation already says "HF donates a proton to water", the equation needs no label.
- A caption exists only to add what the picture cannot say by itself (which lone pair moved; that no proton moved). If it repeats the sentence above, delete it.
- A definition is stated once, in one place. Later parts refer to it, they do not restate it.
- Parallel subsections (Arrhenius, Brønsted-Lowry, Lewis) share a shape, not a script. The same slot in each should say something different, or the slot goes.

## Words

Hers: simply, essentially, note, e.g., memorize these, do not confuse, for which, the trick is, recipe, box (Kw-Box, K-Box), ladder, rung, tier, deck.

Not hers: crucial, powerful, elegant, honest, unlock, journey, dive, explore, master, delve, "let's", "it's worth noting", "in other words" (say it right the first time), "key takeaway", "fundamental" as filler. No exclamation marks.

## Her sentences, usable as is

- "The difference between CA and CB in a conjugate A/B pair is one proton (H⁺)."
- "Strength indicates the degree of dissociation or fraction dissociated. Do not confuse with concentration."
- "Conjugate ion of a strong (acid or base) is neutral. Conjugate ion of a weak (acid or base) is the opposite weak (base or acid)."
- "All strong acids are equally strong in water; water cannot tell them apart."
- "Hydrolysis: simply a reaction with H₂O."
- "pKa defines the pH at which half of the species has been deprotonated."
- "When Ka or Kb is very small, assume the initial concentration did not change, then use the shortcut. Check the assumption: [H⁺]/[HA]₀ must be under 5 %."

When her sentence exists for the point, use it near-verbatim. Paraphrase loses the voice.

## Test before shipping

Read the paragraph aloud as if to Malcolm across the table.
- Does it open with why? If not, it is not ready.
- Could a sentence be cut with nothing lost? Cut it.
- Does anything appear twice (sentence + equation + caption)? Keep one.
- Is every sentence a sentence?
- Is there a picture where a picture would do the work faster?
- Would she say this word? (See Words.)
