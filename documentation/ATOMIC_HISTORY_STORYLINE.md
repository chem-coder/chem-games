## 1. Democritus and Leucippus: atomos

The ancient Greek philosopher who coined the term "atomos" was Democritus, along with his mentor Leucippus, around 400 BCE.

The word "atomos" literally translates to "uncuttable" or "indivisible" in Greek. Democritus reasoned that if you kept cutting an object in half, you would eventually reach a tiny, fundamental particle that could no longer be divided.

## 2. Aristotle: continuous matter and the four (and five) elements

Aristotle completely rejected the concept of atoms. He strongly opposed Democritus's "atomos" theory and instead argued that matter is continuous and infinitely divisible.

If you cut a piece of matter in half, Aristotle believed you could keep cutting it forever: you would never encounter an "uncuttable" particle.

His arguments against atomic theory focused on two primary concepts.

### The four (and five) elements

Aristotle championed the idea that all matter on Earth is made of varying combinations of four core elements, each possessing a unique pair of qualities:

| Element | Qualities |
|---|---|
| Fire | hot and dry |
| Air | hot and wet |
| Water | cold and wet |
| Earth | cold and dry |

He later added a fifth element called aether (or quintessence), an unchangeable, weightless substance that he believed made up the heavens and celestial bodies.
