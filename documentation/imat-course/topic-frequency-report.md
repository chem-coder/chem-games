## Headline findings

1. **Acids–bases takes sole possession of the lead.** Tied with chemical bonding at 24 total, but the current era isn't close: **12 of the last 75 questions** (2022–26). 2026 asked salt hydrolysis *twice* (NaBr neutral, sodium acetate basic) — exactly pH Lab's Salt Court. The Big Three (bond, acids–bases, stoichiometry) still carry 69 of 199 (35%).

2. **Redox TRIPLED in 2026 — and changed shape.** After 14 years of exactly one question and 2025's two, 2026 asked **three**, and all three are **full equation balancing** (Cu + HNO₃, PbBr₂ + FeBr₃, and a KIO₂ disproportionation) rather than the oxidation-number assignment that defined the topic through 2025. The Ox-State Trainer's half-reaction/balancing side is now the live target, not just its number-assignment tiers. Still zero cell/potential questions in 16 years.

3. **Stoichiometry was absent a SECOND straight year.** The 2025 report predicted regression to the mean; 2026 refused. Two consecutive zeros for the 15-year workhorse now looks like a deliberate rebalancing (its place taken by the redox-balancing cluster, which exercises the same balancing skill). Solutions, matter, periodic table, and equilibrium also sat 2026 out.

4. **Thermochemistry finally scored — the first question in 16 years.** Q47 (exothermic ⇒ products have lower chemical potential energy) is the first entry in a row that had been zero since 2011. One definitional question doesn't make a trend, but the topic is no longer safely skippable.

5. **2025's biomolecule arrival was NOT consolidated** — no biomolecule questions in 2026. The organic trio reverted to classics: pentanones = ketones, ethene = alkene, and C₃H₆O's three functional-group possibilities — all drillable by the shipped organic suite.

6. **2026 was strikingly definitional.** Rate-law-is-experimental, valence electrons of Cl⁻, HClO is an oxyacid, Boyle's law in words, IMF ⇒ boiling point — recall items with almost no computation. Only the three balancing questions require working anything out. Gas laws held steady (1/year since 2023's comeback); kinetics appeared a second consecutive year.

## Question-style notes

- Five options A–E, single best answer, no calculator; every computation resolves to clean mental arithmetic (dilution ratios, integer mole ratios, pH of 10⁻ⁿ solutions, √(10⁻⁸) = 10⁻⁴).
- Recurring stoichiometry patterns: Avogadro-scale atom counting (3×), percentage yield (3×), formula from % composition, limiting reagent.
- Figure-dependent questions are rare (~1–2/year recently, mostly organic structures); nearly everything works as text. Neither 2025 nor 2026 had any in chemistry.

## Data caveats

- 2021–2025 questions and keys were transcribed from image-based PDFs (AcadIMAT shuffled versions, whose keys match the shuffled order); chemistry rows of the keys were re-verified at high resolution, and computable answers were independently solved as cross-checks. **2025's fifteen answers were solved blind first, then checked against the official key when it arrived the same day: 15/15 agreement.**
- **2026 comes from the original unshuffled official publication** (`2026 IMAT Exam (Original Unshuffled).pdf`), in which the correct answer is highlighted — and, as in every original MUR publication, **always printed as option A**. The bank keeps the original option order (answer = A for all 15); the IMAT Decks copies reshuffle each question's options deterministically so the drill can't be gamed. All 15 answers were solved blind first, then confirmed 15/15 against the highlights. The AcadIMAT shuffled version is not yet in the folder.
- Flagged oddities, recorded in the bank's notes: 2012 Q61 prints an unbalanced equation in the original paper (kept verbatim); 2022 Q41's official answer (A) is chemically debatable (CO₂ arguably also qualifies).
- **Rights note:** these are official past-exam questions — fine as private teaching/practice material and as the design oracle (the IMAT Decks drill at `imat-decks/` exists for Malcolm's prep on this basis), but a paid/shipped product must use original questions modeled on them, not verbatim copies.
