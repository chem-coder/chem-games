# GAMSAT chemistry: compiled topic outline

Compiled 2026-10-19 as the source for the GAMSAT column of the SLO matrix.

## Status of this document

ACER, which runs GAMSAT, does not publish a chemistry syllabus. This outline is compiled from three preparation sources and is not an official document. A mark in the GAMSAT column means "listed by the prep sources", not "required by ACER".

Sources (read 2026-10-19):

- **G** = GradReady, <https://gradready.com.au/gamsat-chemistry>
- **I** = gamsat.ie, general chemistry syllabus, <https://www.gamsat.ie/materials/sciences/general-chemistry>. This is where `_teaching-materials/GAMSAT_Chemistry Syllabus.txt` came from.
- **F** = Fraser's Medical, <https://www.frasersmedical.com/blogs/gamsat-syllabus>. It says outright that it is the author's own analysis of past questions, not an official syllabus.

## Exam facts as the sources state them

- Chemistry is about 40% of Section 3 (G and F). G quotes the ACER Information Booklet split as chemistry 40%, biology 40%, physics 20%.
- Section 3 has 75 four-option multiple-choice questions in 150 minutes (G). That is roughly 30 chemistry-influenced questions.
- Level: first-year university chemistry (G); university-level knowledge (F).
- The exam tests applying principles, not recalling a textbook (G, F). Needed values are usually printed in the question; no periodic table or calculator is provided (G).
- Question styles (G): most plausible account; graph reading (titration curves, rate plots); table reading (pKa, Keq, melting points); a rule taught in the question and then applied; matching a rule to a skeletal formula; multi-select statements; spatial reasoning (3D shapes, projections, Lewis diagrams); estimating mole ratios and limiting reagents without a calculator.

## Topic outline

Tags show which sources list the topic. Topics with all three tags are the safest 1s; a single tag is thinner evidence.

### 1. Atoms and the periodic table
- Atomic structure, nuclear structure [G, I, F]
- Periodic table and how properties change across it [G, F]
- Effective nuclear charge and shielding [G]
- Electron configuration [F]

### 2. Bonding and structure
- Metallic, covalent, ionic and hydrogen bonding; intermolecular bonding [G, I, F]
- Electronegativity and bond polarity; comparing molecules by polarity [G, I]
- Lewis diagrams and VSEPR [G]; molecules and shape [F]
- Sigma and pi bonds [G]; hybridization [I]
- Hydrogen bonding in organic systems [I]

### 3. Stoichiometry
- Balancing equations; conservation [G, F]
- Mole ratios and limiting reagents, estimated by hand [G]

### 4. Oxidation numbers and electrochemistry
- Oxidation numbers in inorganic and organic chemistry [I, F]
- Redox half-equations; assigning oxidation and reduction [G, F]
- Using electrode potentials to predict reactions; Nernst equation [I, F]
- Redox equilibria; electrode potentials in biological systems [I]

### 5. Gases, liquids, solids, solutions
- Gas law PV = nRT, rearranged and interpreted [G]; gas behaviour [F]
- Liquid and solid behaviour [F]
- Solubility curves and phase diagrams [I]
- Colligative properties: freezing point depression, osmotic pressure and osmolarity, finding a protein's molar mass [I, F]
- Raoult's law [I]
- Solubility and Ksp calculations [I, F]

### 6. Thermodynamics
- Types of energy; internal energy, heat and work; laws of thermodynamics [F]
- Enthalpy, entropy, Gibbs free energy [G, I, F]
- Using ΔG = ΔH − TΔS to predict a reaction [I]
- Gibbs free energy profiles [I]
- Chemical potential and its biological uses [I]

### 7. Kinetics
- Rate equation, rate constant, reaction order, determining the rate law [G, I, F]
- Rate-limiting step; relating the rate law to the mechanism [G, I]
- Arrhenius equation, activation energy, link to biological mechanism [I]
- Enzyme kinetics and the Michaelis-Menten equation [I]

### 8. Equilibrium
- Equilibrium expression; K versus Q; Kc, Kp, Ksp [F]
- Le Chatelier's principle and its applications [I, F]

### 9. Acids and bases
- Definitions of acids and bases; strength [G, F]
- Links between pH, pKa, periodic trends and electronegativity [G]
- Equilibria applied: acid-base equilibria, indicators, pKa use [I, F]
- Buffers; Henderson-Hasselbalch; buffering in blood; proteins and other biological systems [I, F]

### 10. Organic structure and nomenclature
- Nomenclature, IUPAC, skeletal formulae [G, F]
- Families of molecules and the main functional groups, and their consistent behaviour [G, F]
- Non-standard naming [F]

### 11. Isomerism and stereochemistry
- Structural, geometric and stereoisomerism; conformations; enantiomers; chirality [G, F]
- Projections: Fischer, wedge and dash [G]
- Prochirality and inorganic isomerism [F]
- Benzene, and how substituents affect it [F]

### 12. Organic reactions
- Functional groups and their reactions; basic reactions [G, F]
- Substitution and elimination logic: SN1, SN2, E1, E2 [G]
- Reactions of benzene [F]
- Modifying functionality; rearrangements [F]

### 13. Biochemistry
- Protein, fat and carbohydrate synthesis [G]
- Biochemistry basics and reactions in biology [F]
- Citric acid cycle; overlap with biology [G]

### 14. Analytical methods
- Chromatography and separations [G, F]
- Reading spectra: NMR, mass-spec style data, infrared (basic and advanced) [G, F]
- Comparing unknowns from printed results [G]
